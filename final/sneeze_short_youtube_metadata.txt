DESCRIPTION
A man in his 30s held in a sneeze while driving: he pinched his nose and clamped his mouth shut. The trapped blast of air tore a tiny hole in his windpipe (trachea). His neck swelled up, and doctors could feel air crackling under his skin. Luckily it healed on its own, without surgery, in about five weeks.

Doctors say it's the first known case of its kind. So next time a sneeze is coming, let it out (into a tissue or your elbow).

Source: BMJ Case Reports (2023), a case treated at Ninewells Hospital, Dundee, Scotland. Covered by Gizmodo, Interesting Engineering and others.
