DESCRIPTION
An Australian man in his 40s was dying of heart failure, and no donor heart was available. In November 2024, surgeons at St Vincent's Hospital in Sydney replaced his heart with the BiVACOR Total Artificial Heart: a titanium pump with just one moving part, a rotor held floating by magnets, so there are no valves or bearings to wear out. It pumps blood to both the body and the lungs.

He lived with it for more than 100 days, and in February 2025 he became the first person in the world to walk out of hospital with this device. In March 2025, a donor heart became available and he had a successful transplant. The device was invented by Queensland engineer Daniel Timms, who started working on it after his father died of heart failure. It is still in clinical trials.

Sources: St Vincent's Hospital Sydney / Monash University announcement (March 2025), reported by CNN and ZME Science.
