DESCRIPTION
A man in his 40s spent about eight months on a strict carnivore diet, by his own account eating large daily amounts of cheese, butter and fatty beef. He said he felt great: more energy, a clearer head, and weight loss. Then painless yellow nodules appeared on his palms, soles and elbows. Blood tests found his total cholesterol had shot past 1000 mg/dL, roughly five times the healthy threshold of under 200 mg/dL. Doctors diagnosed cutaneous xanthoma: visible deposits of lipid-laden cells pushed into the skin because his blood was carrying far more fat than his body could clear.

Extremely high cholesterol like this is strongly linked to accelerated plaque buildup in the arteries (atherosclerosis), the process behind most heart attacks and strokes. The case was published in JAMA Cardiology in January 2025 (correction issued August 2025 clarifying the diagnosis as xanthoma, not xanthelasma).
