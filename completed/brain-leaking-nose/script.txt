Her brain was leaking out of her nose, and doctors blamed allergies.
In 2013, Kendra's face hit a dashboard in a crash.
Her nose then ran nonstop for years.
Then a specialist tested the fluid. It wasn't mucus.
It was the liquid your brain floats in.
The crash had cracked a tiny hole in her skull.
She lost half a pint of brain fluid a day.
That hole was an open door for bacteria into her brain.
Surgeons went up her nostril and plugged it with her own fat.
If your runny nose is one-sided and salty, get it checked.
