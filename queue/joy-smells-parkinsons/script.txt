She could smell Parkinson's disease before doctors could.
Joy Milne, a Scottish nurse, noticed her husband's scent change.
A musky odor appeared ten years before his diagnosis.
Scientists tested her with T-shirts worn by sick and healthy people.
She picked every Parkinson's shirt, plus one healthy person.
Eight months later, that person was diagnosed too.
The scent comes from sebum, the oily wax in your skin.
Researchers built a three-minute skin swab test from her discovery.
In lab trials, it was about ninety-five percent accurate.
Her nose may help catch Parkinson's years earlier, saving lives.
