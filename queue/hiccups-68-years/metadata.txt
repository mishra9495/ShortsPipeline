DESCRIPTION
Charles Osborne, an Iowa man, started hiccuping in 1922 after a fall on a Nebraska farm while handling a hog, and did not stop for about 68 years. Doctors think the fall burst a tiny blood vessel in his brain and damaged the part of the brain stem that normally switches the hiccup reflex off. His diaphragm spasmed roughly 20 to 40 times a minute, an estimated 430 million hiccups. He still married twice and fathered eight children. The hiccups stopped suddenly in 1990, for reasons nobody knows, and he died in 1991 aged 97. Guinness World Records lists it as the longest attack of hiccups.

Most hiccups pass in minutes. Hiccups that last more than 48 hours are worth a call to your doctor.
