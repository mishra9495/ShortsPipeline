DESCRIPTION
A man arrested for drunk driving had not touched alcohol in about 20 years. The reason: yeast in his gut was fermenting carbohydrates into ethanol, a rare condition called auto-brewery syndrome (gut fermentation syndrome).

In a documented 2019 case, a 45-year-old man had two drunk-driving arrests with blood alcohol two to three times the legal limit, and a breathalyzer reading of 0.41% on the second. Doctors found Saccharomyces cerevisiae (brewer's yeast) in his stool and other fermenting microbes in his gut. A monitored carbohydrate challenge, with no alcohol allowed, sent his blood alcohol above 400 mg/dl. An eight-week course of the antifungal micafungin made his alcohol undetectable.

The condition is rare and likely underdiagnosed, and has no standard diagnostic criteria. A monitored sugar challenge is the key test.

Source: "A Case of Auto-brewery Syndrome Treated with Micafungin", Cureus 2019 (PMC6853272). Also StatPearls, Auto-Brewery Syndrome (NBK513346).
