DESCRIPTION
Brent Chapman lost his sight at 13 after a rare, severe reaction to ibuprofen (Stevens-Johnson syndrome). For about 20 years, cornea transplants couldn't restore his vision. So in 2025, surgeons in Vancouver tried a last-resort operation called osteo-odonto-keratoprosthesis, better known as "tooth-in-eye" surgery.

They removed his canine tooth with a thin layer of jawbone, shaped it into a small block, and drilled a plastic optical lens through it. The tooth-and-lens was sewn into his cheek for a few months to grow its own blood supply, then implanted into his eye. Because it's his own tissue, his body is less likely to reject it. It worked: he can see again, and says he and his surgeon made eye contact.

Source: Dr. Greg Moloney's team, Mount Saint Joseph Hospital, Vancouver, the first tooth-in-eye surgeries in Canada (2025). Covered by CBC, CTV, NBC and Smithsonian.
