DESCRIPTION
Kendra Jackson of Omaha, Nebraska was rear-ended in a 2013 car crash and her face hit the dashboard. Afterwards her nose ran constantly for years, with headaches, and doctors kept telling her it was allergies.

In 2018, specialists at Nebraska Medicine tested the fluid. It was cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), the clear liquid your brain and spinal cord float in. The crash had left a tiny hole in the base of her skull, right above her nasal cavity, and she was losing about half a pint of CSF a day through her nose. A leak like that is dangerous because it gives bacteria a direct route to the brain (meningitis).

Surgeons repaired it endoscopically, going up through her nostril and plugging the hole with fatty tissue taken from her own body, no open brain surgery needed. She was expected to make a full recovery.

Real signs of a CSF leak: clear watery drainage from one nostril, often worse when bending forward, with a salty or metallic taste, especially after a head injury. If that sounds like you, see a doctor.
