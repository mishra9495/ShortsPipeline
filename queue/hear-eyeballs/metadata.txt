DESCRIPTION
Stephen Mabbutt, a 57-year-old from Charlton near Banbury, England, could hear his own eyeballs moving. Every time he looked around he heard a scratchy noise "like sandpaper rubbed on wood". His heartbeat thumped in his ear and chewing became deafening.

A CT scan found the cause: a tiny hole in the bone over the balance canal of his inner ear, a rare condition called superior canal dehiscence syndrome (SCDS). The gap lets sounds from inside the body reach the inner ear far too loudly. Surgeons sealed it using a piece of his own bone, and the noises stopped. "It's made a big difference to my life," he said.

Sources: Live Science, Fox News and Shortlist coverage of the 2011 case (treated by consultant Richard Irving); SCDS overview: Ward et al., "Superior Canal Dehiscence Syndrome: Lessons from the First 20 Years", Frontiers in Neurology (2017).
