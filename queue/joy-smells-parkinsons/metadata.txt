DESCRIPTION
Joy Milne, a retired nurse from Perth, Scotland, noticed her husband Les's smell change about ten years before he was diagnosed with Parkinson's disease. When she later mentioned it to researchers, they tested her with T-shirts worn by people with and without Parkinson's. She picked out the Parkinson's shirts, and also flagged one person from the healthy group who was diagnosed about eight months later.

The smell comes from sebum, the oily wax the skin makes. Chemists at the University of Manchester used her nose as a guide and developed a skin swab test using mass spectrometry that gives results in about three minutes and was about 95% accurate in laboratory conditions (2022). It is still being validated and is not yet a clinical test.

Sources: University of Manchester news, "'Sniffing out' Parkinson's: Three-minute skin swab breakthrough" (7 Sept 2022); Parkinson's UK, "Researchers move closer to a test that can sniff out Parkinson's"; ACS Central Science (2019) "Sebum as a source of Parkinson's disease biomarkers".
