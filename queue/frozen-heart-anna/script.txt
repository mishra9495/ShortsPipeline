Her heart stopped for hours, and she lived.
In 1999, skier Anna Bagenholm fell through a frozen Norwegian stream.
Trapped, she found an air pocket and breathed.
Forty minutes later, the cold stopped her heart.
Her body temperature fell to thirteen point seven degrees Celsius.
A heart-lung machine drew out her blood, warmed it, and returned it.
After two and a half hours, her heart beat again.
The cold had slowed her brain, so it barely needed oxygen.
She woke ten days later, nearly fully recovered.
Doctors now say nobody is dead until they are warm and dead.
