DESCRIPTION
In May 1999, 29-year-old skier Anna Bagenholm fell through the ice of a frozen stream in Norway and was trapped under it for about 80 minutes. She found an air pocket and kept breathing for roughly 40 minutes, then the freezing water stopped her heart. When she reached the hospital her body temperature was just 13.7 degrees Celsius (normal is about 37). Doctors connected her to a heart-lung machine that warmed her blood outside her body. Her heart started beating again after 2 hours and 35 minutes without a beat. She woke ten days later, paralyzed at first, and went on to recover almost completely. Doctors say the cold slowed her brain so much it needed very little oxygen, which is why the old medical saying holds: nobody is dead until they are warm and dead.

Sources: Gilbert M et al., "Resuscitation from accidental hypothermia of 13.7 C with circulatory arrest", The Lancet 2000; 355(9201):375-376; Wikipedia, "Anna Bagenholm"; ScienceAlert, "This woman survived the lowest body temperature ever recorded".
