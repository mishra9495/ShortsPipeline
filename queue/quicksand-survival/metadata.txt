DESCRIPTION
Movies got quicksand wrong. In 2005, physicist Daniel Bonn's team (University of Amsterdam) published a study in Nature showing that quicksand is about twice as dense as the human body (roughly 2 g/mL vs 1 g/mL). That means you float: a person sinks only to about the waist and no further.

The real danger is getting stuck. Once the sand settles around your legs it packs tight, and the researchers estimated that pulling a foot out at just 1 cm per second takes about the same force as lifting a medium-size car. Bonn warned that friends yanking hard on you could do serious damage.

The escape: wiggle your legs slowly in small circles so water seeps into the gap and loosens the sand, then lean back and spread out so your body's buoyancy floats your legs up. Be patient, but don't wait around near the coast: quicksand is mostly found near the sea, and the incoming tide is what can actually drown you.

Source: Khaldoun, Eiser, Wegdam & Bonn, "Liquefaction of quicksand under stress", Nature 437, 635 (2005). Covered by National Geographic and Nature News.
